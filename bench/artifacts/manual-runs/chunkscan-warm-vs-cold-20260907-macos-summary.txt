Reading it
----------

The counters are the result; the seconds are the consequence.

* wide and small-page isolate the unconditional cache merge. The disk walk is
  already column-pruned by PR #23 on both sides (dirs barely move), so the
  drop from 60416 to 1443 and from 960512 to 7755 large-chunk merges is the
  fix on its own. Before, every page re-inserted all 60000 resident chunks:
  1024 large chunks x 59 pages = 60416, and x 938 pages = 960512. After, a
  page merges only the large chunks it visits -- 24.5 and 8.3 per page.
  small-page pays that per-page cost 16x more often, which is why the same
  world shows the larger factor there.

* narrow is a world exactly one large-chunk column wide (grid width 8 =
  large_chunk_width_chunks), so no column can ever be skipped and the
  column-level test of PR #23 pruned nothing at all: 625 large chunks x 40
  pages = 25000, listed and merged in full on every page. The per-large-chunk
  test cuts by y inside the column and brings both counters to 8538, and the
  warm walk from 1.58 s to 0.60 s.

Caveats
-------

* cold_s is a full enumeration against the filesystem and is the noisy column
  here. It is not a controlled comparison in this session: populate_s, which
  is identical code on both sides, was itself 12% higher in the narrow-after
  block (10.14 s vs 9.09 s) and 8% lower in the wide-after block, so the cold
  deltas track machine state, not the change. The after side does strictly
  less work than the before side on the cold walk too -- the dirs counter says
  so exactly -- so read cold_s as a scale reference, and read the comparison
  off warm_s and the counters.

* This is not the pair of numbers issue #26 quotes (warm 1.226 s vs cold
  0.270 s). That was a single first page with limit 1024 on a 200k-chunk
  world, where the cold side reads only 1024 chunk states and the dir walk
  dominates. This benchmark walks the whole world, so both sides pay 40-60k
  populated-state reads and cold never comes out ahead of warm. The mechanism
  behind the issue's inversion -- a warm page paying O(resident chunks) on top
  of the disk walk -- is what the merge counters measure directly, and it is
  gone.

* macOS/APFS only. Linux/ext4 and Windows were not measured. The experimental
  fs_region_v1 layout is not covered: it keeps its full .rgn walk and this
  benchmark only exercises fs_split_v1.
